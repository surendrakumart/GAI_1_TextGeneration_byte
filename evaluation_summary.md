# Qualitative Evaluation Summary

The 20 outputs were reviewed qualitatively for instruction-following, coherence, coverage of requested elements, clarity, formatting, and obvious failure modes. They cover several styles, including explanatory prose, a story, a dialogue, an email, a summary, and a structured study plan.

Overall, the outputs are readable and generally follow the requested formats. Most educational responses use clear headings and examples; structured responses include requested lists or tables; the microfiction and dialogue maintain recognizable narrative or conversational forms; and the summarization response remains below the 50-word limit. The audience-adaptation sample changes the level of technical detail while keeping the basic cloud-computing explanation consistent.

Potential failure modes include generic recommendations, simplified explanations that omit advanced nuance, and factual statements that would need independent verification in a production or academic setting. A few educational analogies are illustrative rather than exact models of the underlying phenomena. The task-prioritization plan also assumes a particular available time window, which is explicitly stated in the response. The alternative-history response is framed as speculative rather than historical fact.

No BLEU or perplexity score is reported. BLEU would require a suitable reference text or reference set, and perplexity would require access to model probabilities or a compatible evaluation pipeline. Neither was available for the hosted ChatGPT interface used to generate these samples. This is a qualitative sample review, not a benchmark or independent factual audit.
